1.	6 DOF biped overview
Balance
 - It can stand and balance in a simple 2D setup
Walking
 - It can walk forward with basic and somewhat stiff motion
Changing direction, very limited
It lacks roll and yaw so it cannot turn naturally
Why the limitation:
 - Motion mostly in one plane forward and backward
 - No lateral control leads to weak sideways balance
 - No hip rotation means no proper turning
Conclusion
 - 6 DOF is good for basic walking
 - Not enough for realistic movement or turning
2.	6 DOF biped controller
Inputs
 - Joint positions q
 - Joint velocities dq
 - Optional foot contact
Outputs
 - Joint torques or target positions
Core idea
 - The controller keeps the robot close to desired joint angles
 - tau equals Kp times position error plus Kd times velocity error
 - This is a PD controller
Main components
 - Posture control: keeps torso upright and stable
 - Joint tracking: each joint follows a reference angle
Simple gait generator
 - Alternates legs
 - Defines swing and stance phases
Walking logic
 - One leg is support
 - One leg is swing
 - Controller keeps support leg stable
 - Controller moves swing leg forward
 - Legs switch periodically
Balance strategy
 - Keep center of mass above support foot
 - Adjust torso angle slightly
 - Use ankle and hip to correct errors
Limitations
 - No lateral balance
 - Sensitive to disturbances
 - Turning is weak
What it is good for
 - Standing balance
 - Forward walking
Summary
 - PD control per joint
 - Predefined walking pattern
 - Minimal balance logic
 - Works only in simplified scenarios

 
3.	Improving balance
Add hip roll and ankle roll on both legs
 - Hip roll
	Allows body to shift left and right
	Very important for lateral balance
 - Ankle roll
	Corrects sideways tilt
	Helps keep center of mass above foot
 - Hip yaw
	Useful for turning later
	Not required for basic balance
Why this helps
 - With only sagittal motion
	Forward and backward balance is possible
	Sideways balance is weak
 - With roll DOF
	Weight can shift between legs
	Exoskeleton can react to lateral falling
	Standing becomes more realistic
Additional improvements
 - IMU
	Measures torso angle and angular velocity
 - Foot contact sensing
	Detects support leg
 - State machine
	Includes standing weight shift swing and landing
Center of mass control
 - Keeps center of mass above support area
Controller improvements
 - Torso stabilization
	Keeps upper body upright
 - Weight shift control
	Moves body over support leg
 - Foot placement control
	Places foot to maintain balance

5.	AI and ML improvements for the exoskeleton
AI is used to improve control performance, not replace the controller
It is integrated on top of the existing control architecture
Main roles of AI
 - Improve balance by generating corrective actions
 - Adapt gait parameters such as step length and timing
 - Adjust posture based on disturbances
 - Improve walking smoothness and efficiency
Types of AI functionality
 - Reference generator
	AI outputs desired joint angles or trajectories
 - Balance correction
	AI outputs small adjustments to stabilize the system
 - Gait adaptation
	AI modifies walking pattern based on current state
 - State estimation support
	AI can estimate missing or noisy sensor data
Model requirements for edge deployment
 - Small model size
 - Low latency inference
 - Fixed input and output structure
 - Prefer simple feedforward networks
Training process
 - Use MuJoCo simulation to generate training data
 - Train model using reinforcement learning or supervised learning
 - Validate model in simulation under different conditions
Deployment process
 - Export trained model
 - Convert to format usable in C++
 - Optimize for embedded hardware
 - Run inference on Zynq platform
Integration with controller
 - AI outputs reference values or corrections
 - PD controller tracks references
 - Controller ensures stability and safety
Limitations
 - AI should not directly control torques at this stage
 - Performance depends on training quality
 - Requires validation for real world transfer
